## The method

1. **Find what's examined.** List the tutorial questions and slide exercises, group them into topics in the order a beginner needs them, and drop anything none of them touch.
2. **One idea at a time.** Every idea in the notes is a short rule, a diagram, then an "apply it" question with the answer hidden.
3. **Test, then mix.** Each topic ends with a test in the exam's formats. Then mixed questions across topics, because the real exam isn't sorted by chapter.
4. **Log the confusion.** Every question I get stuck on goes in a log under its topic, so I reread exactly what tripped me up.

## SSD_Lock-In

EEEN20070 Solid State Devices: 14 topics, from the bond model through to pn junction diodes, end-of-topic tests built on the tutorial questions, two mock midterms and a formula sheet.

## DE_Lock-In

Digital Electronics, Lectures 1 to 10. This one turned into a small quiz site on GitHub Pages: you fill in K-maps and truth tables by clicking, type Boolean expressions, and trace values through adders, decoders and muxes. It tests a typed expression on every row of the truth table, so any correct form is accepted, and it rejects answers that are right but not minimal. Every diagram is drawn from code, and a check script re-proves every answer before I push.
